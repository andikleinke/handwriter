# Handwriter — audit brief (2026-10-17)

*Prepared for an outside review. The reviewer has researched how Apple's
"hello" welcome animation (first boot of a new Mac) is made and is asked to
recommend a new approach for Handwriter in that light. This document is the
honest history: the goal, the architecture, the problem that has never gone
away, everything that was tried against it, what was measured, and what we
believe the structural reasons are. Code and history are at
<https://github.com/andikleinke/handwriter>; the running app is at
<https://andikleinke.github.io/handwriter/>.*

---

## 1. The goal

Animate text being handwritten in a browser: a pen that writes a word in a
calligraphic or cursive hand, as a transparent movie (ProRes 4444 / HEVC with
alpha) for use in Keynote and other projects.

The quality bar, set by the owner and never reached:

- **script-font evenness** — the letters must look as even and designed as a
  professionally drawn script face (the target image is "hello" in Pinyon
  Script; Apple's welcome "hello" is the same idea);
- **a natural pen** — continuous smooth strokes, a visible nib, human rhythm
  (ease in/out, faster on hairlines, slower in curves), a plausible stroke
  order;
- **no junction artefacts** — nothing may pop in, bulge, notch, serrate or
  lag where strokes cross, where a stroke retraces itself, or where one letter
  joins the next. The owner's formulation, repeated several times: *"if a line
  is 8 px wide before a joint and 8 px wide after it, it is 8 px wide all the
  way through the joint, in the same direction; an irregular section between
  two smooth ones is by definition what needs smoothing."*

Fonts in use: Pinyon Script (OFL, pointed-pen calligraphic, letters connect by
design), Autography (commercial, monoline brush script, letters do **not**
connect), Ms Madi (OFL, monoline connected script, added 2026-10-17).

## 2. Where the project stands (v0.26.0)

Plain JavaScript + Vite, ~9 k lines, no framework. Two routes existed; one
remains.

**The font route (the only route since v0.23.0).** The letters are a font's
own outlines; the pen is *inferred* from them and the animation *uncovers*
the outline along the inferred pen path.

1. `HarfBuzz` (wasm) shapes the text into positioned glyphs.
2. Each glyph is rasterised (512 px/em by default) and thinned (Zhang–Suen)
   into a **skeleton**, then a graph: endpoints and junctions as nodes, runs as
   edges; short spurs pruned, junction knots contracted (`src/lib/graph.js`,
   `strokeRecovery.js`).
3. A **beam search** over the graph chooses the pen order: which edge first,
   which direction, where to retrace, where to lift
   (`src/lib/strokeOrder.js`, ~1 400 lines of weighted terms: start top-left,
   rightward drift, downstrokes on shades for pointed pens, template fit to
   taught cursive letterforms, penalties for extra strokes, retraces, climbing
   a stem to reach a crossbar, …). Results are cached per glyph
   (`RECORD_VERSION` bumps invalidate) and can be **hand-edited** in a
   per-letter editor (drag/split/delete points, draw a stroke; undo/redo;
   save/load a "hand").
4. **Pen plan** (`src/lib/penPlan.js`): strokes become pen-down chains;
   between letters a **bridge** (Hermite curve with the exit/entry tangents)
   is planned where the tail of one stroke can flow into the head of the next;
   untravelled tails/heads are hidden; pointed pens get lift-joins.
5. **Timing** (`src/lib/timing.js`): arc length ↔ time with a speed model
   (curvature-dependent, ease in/out).
6. **Reveal** (`src/render/sweep.js`, ~1 100 lines, and
   `src/render/animator.js`): the thing that decides, for every raster pixel
   of the outline, *which pass of the pen* reveals it and *at which arc
   length*. Since v0.21.0 the strokes are additionally drawn as **vector
   bands** (centreline ± measured cross-section, clipped by the outline), with
   the raster map as a fallback for what the bands do not cover.
7. **Export**: 60 fps frames posted to a Vite plugin that encodes a `.mov`
   with alpha through AVFoundation (Mac only). Static hosting on GitHub Pages
   (auto-deploy on push); movie export needs the local dev server.

**The pen model (retired 2026-10-17, last in tag v0.22.0)** was an
independent route: letters written from a taught letter grammar (dense
waypoints per letter in an x-height frame), Hobby splines, a kinematic pace
model, a simulated nib with pressure, joins composed geometrically. It had no
junction problems at all — it *is* a pen — but its letterforms could not get
close to a drawn script face's evenness ("very jagged and uneven"), and the
owner retired it.

## 3. The problem that never went away

Every version since the font route began has had artefacts **where ink
belongs to more than one pass of the pen**:

- **crossings** (the `J`'s loop through its stem, the `z`, the `x`, every
  `e`'s eye): a bite out of the first stroke where the second will cross, or
  a piece of the second stroke shown early, or a nub/notch on the first;
- **retraces** (the `o`'s right side written twice, the `d`'s stem, a `J`'s
  bar out and back): a ragged seam between the two passes, "chopped-off
  chunks" that fill in later;
- **merged entries** (a letter's entry hairline running along the bottom of
  its own eye; a hairline running inside a shade): the first pass reveals a
  fat blob that belongs to the later pass, or the thin pass is drawn as a
  crumbly thin strip inside the fat ink;
- **joins between letters**: the bridge meets the letters at an angle, with a
  step in width at either end, a notch at the corner, bits of the hidden tail
  showing with raster edges. (Screenshots from 2026-10-17 evening, after
  v0.26.0, still show a jag at the pen's front where the bridge lands on the
  `e` in Autography.)

Each fix removed the instance it targeted and the next letter, or the next
font, showed the same family of defect somewhere else. The owner's reading,
which we share: *the reveal-map approach is a dead end*; we have been
"fixing smudges on every single letter of every new alphabet".

## 4. What was tried, in order

The versions are git tags; each entry says what the mechanism was, what it
fixed, what it broke or left.

| Version | Mechanism | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ v0.12 | Reveal by a growing **disc** of the nib's radius along the pen path; first pass to touch a pixel claims it. | Shaded strokes wider than the inscribed disc leave ink unclaimed → picked up by the next pass → "chunks" break out of shades and fill in at the crossing. |
| v0.14 | **Pointed-pen rules** in the stroke order (shades down, hairlines up, lift-joins, entry/exit hairlines, per-letter continuity for a d g q). | Stroke order on Pinyon became calligraphically right; join checks pass on alphabet + pangram. Artefacts unchanged in kind. |
| v0.15 | Nib radius from a **running 75th percentile** of the distance map (crossings inflate a minority of the window); **wedge rule**: unclaimed ink between two passes goes to the later one. Reveal by the **measured cross-section** (march along the normal to the ink's edge or to the *valley* between two cores) instead of the disc. | Late ink on z 568→59 px, f 218→0. Measured: a swept pen reproduces Pinyon's outline only to ~17 % XOR (12 % missing, 5 % extra), so "just draw it with a pen" was ruled out for a shaded face. |
| v0.17 | Give a retrace the arc of the pass it goes over (settles the `o`'s seam). | The later pass's *own* new ink then appeared early (the `o`'s exit swirl drawn on the upstroke). **Reverted** in v0.17.1. Also tried and rejected: median filters over the arc field; several caps and valley-rule variants (A/B'd pixel-identical). |
| v0.19 | Provenance per pixel (which mechanism claimed it) → found that at a shallow crossing there is no valley, so the reading runs into the other stroke, one-sided. **Cap the facing side** at the entering width; leftover ink joins the nearest *centreline*. | `L` nub 356→2 px, `J`/`t` visibly clean at magnification; Pinyon unchanged. The owner: "it's not fixed" — the `J` still had a notch mid-stem and a nub at the top. |
| v0.20 | Through a short crossing, **redraw the centreline** (Hermite between clean entry and exit) and **blend the width** from the width brought in to the width taken out; long stretches keep the entering width then a smoothed reading; cut stretches at hairpins/corners; a pointed pen's upstroke hairline stays thin inside a shade; fractional pixel coverage at band edges with a **second owner** that completes the pixel. | Late ink J 53→1, L 15→0, Pinyon hello 628→392, "quick fox" 1358→637. The `J`'s stem measured 21–22 px on every row through the crossing. At the owner's zoom the step was still visible: a raster mask has no outline to clip against inside a junction. |
| v0.21 | **Vector bands**: every stroke drawn as a polygon (centreline ± measured half-widths), clipped by the glyph outline, over the raster layer; joins as unclipped bands; leftover ink to the later stroke within reach. | Junction edges are lines, not pixel steps. The `J` is clean at 1:1. New family: joins. |
| v0.22 | Crossbar rule in the stroke order (a climb back up a stem to reach a bar costs; the bar is its own stroke). Ms Madi bundled. | The `J` no longer climbs its stem; its overshoot blob (pen never visited it) gone. |
| v0.23 | Pen model removed. | — |
| v0.24 | A join is **one band** from the last cross-section of the letter it leaves to the first of the letter it reaches, tapering, painted under the letters; round nib front. | Better at the owner's zoom but still "three pieces". |
| v0.25 | Every plan item starts its own junction leg; a letter's entry takes its width from readings that reach background (no nib-radius floor, which reads merged ink); bridge widths run linearly between the strokes. | Profile through h→e is a straight 19→15 px taper — and the picture still showed lumps. |
| v0.26 | The lumps were the **raster fallback**, not the bands: within 0.1 em of a bridge the raster is erased until the pen has passed; the corridor is the bands' alone. | Clean on our 3× crops of the live preview; the owner's screenshot the same evening still shows a jag at the pen front at the `e`. |

Other things measured and rejected along the way: median filters on the arc
field (no effect / tripled the jumps); interpolating widths across junctions
anchored on neighbours (inherits inflation) or on minima (starves shades);
capping both sides (starves pointed-pen shades); a wider "later stroke takes
the fillet" reach (no visual gain, metrics worse); a raster lag behind the
bands everywhere (slivers along shade edges until the raster catches up).

## 5. What we know, quantitatively

- **Metrics** (`scripts/inspect.js`, run in the page): *late ink* = pixels
  under a stroke's own band at some sample that are revealed more than
  0.15 em of arc later (or by a later chain); *pop-in* = pixels revealed more
  than 0.22 em from any sample within 0.05 em of their own arc. These are
  raster-map metrics; since v0.21 they no longer describe what is drawn where
  bands cover the ink, so visual judgement on crops of the **live preview
  canvas at device pixels** became the real test. Offscreen renders at other
  scales repeatedly hid what the owner saw.
- Cross-session numbers are not baselines; several phantom regressions were
  chased before adopting same-session `git stash` A/Bs.
- Sweep vs outline (v0.15): a variable-width sweep of the recovered path
  reproduces Pinyon's outline to 17.7 % XOR at best settings; 13 % for the
  best letters, 39 % for `s`. Not measured for monoline faces (Autography,
  Ms Madi), where it would be far smaller.
- Width readings at stroke ends are of the *merged* ink; the planner's
  end-width (min over the last 0.05 em) and the sweep's reading at the cut
  disagree by 2×. Readings quantise at 0.5 px and overshoot the edge by
  ~0.25 px on average; a 1 px error is a visible step at 4–5× magnification,
  which is the scale the owner reviews at.

## 6. Why we think it keeps failing (structural)

1. **The letterform is an outline, not a stroke.** A script face's outline is
   the *union* of ink from several pen passes, drawn by a designer with
   overlaps, fillets, terminal flicks and a contrast that a real pen only
   approximates. Everything we do is reverse engineering: skeleton →
   pen order → widths → ownership of every pixel. Each stage is a heuristic
   with 0.5–1 px uncertainty, and junctions are exactly where all the
   uncertainties meet.
2. **Pixel ownership is the wrong primitive for a join.** Deciding, for each
   raster pixel, which pass reveals it, produces boundaries decided pixel by
   pixel from measurements taken sample by sample. We moved the boundaries to
   vector bands, but the bands' *widths* are still those measurements, and the
   raster fallback remains underneath.
3. **Two sources of truth.** The outline (clip) and the band (measured width)
   never agree to better than a pixel; on a free edge the clip hides it, in a
   junction nothing does. Every "fix" since v0.19 has been about which of the
   two wins where.
4. **The pen order is inferred, so it is sometimes wrong in ways no reveal
   can hide** (the `J` climbing its stem; retraced bars; entries that start
   inside a crossing). The hand editor exists for this, but the owner does
   not want per-letter work on every alphabet.
5. **Fonts that do not connect** (Autography) need an invented connector; a
   real pen would lift. We bridge them because the owner wants continuous
   writing; the bridge is then a guess at a stroke the designer never drew.

## 7. What Apple's "hello" suggests (hypotheses for the reviewer)

We have not analysed Apple's implementation; the reviewer has. From the
outside it looks like the opposite order of operations from ours: the
**stroke is the design**. A single designed centreline (or a few), a designed
width profile along it, revealed by trimming the path — no outline to
reconcile, no pixel to own, no junction to arbitrate because at a crossing
the second stroke simply paints over the first. The letterform is whatever
that stroke leaves behind. If that is right, then:

- our v0.15 measurement (17 % XOR against Pinyon's outline) is the cost of
  insisting on a *given font's outline* as the result. With a monoline or
  designed stroke face the cost is near zero;
- the whole reveal engine (`sweep.js`, the maps, the fallbacks, the junction
  rules) disappears, replaced by: a path, a width function, a trim, a cap;
- what remains hard is getting a good centreline + width profile per letter,
  which is either (a) designed by hand (Apple's way, presumably), (b)
  recovered from a monoline font where it is nearly exact, or (c) recovered
  from a shaded font and accepted as "in the spirit of" rather than
  "identical to" the face. Our skeleton + width recovery already does (b)/(c)
  to within the figures above, and the hand editor could become the design
  tool for (a).

## 8. Questions we would like the audit to answer

1. Given Apple's approach, should Handwriter stop uncovering outlines and
   render **the stroke itself** (centreline + width profile + round/designed
   caps, trimmed along arc length)? If yes, what is the recommended
   representation and renderer (Canvas 2D polygons as now, SVG
   `stroke-dasharray`, Core Animation `strokeEnd` in a native app, a signed
   distance field…)?
2. For a shaded (pointed-pen) look, how is the width profile best obtained —
   from the font's outline (our recovery), from a pressure model along the
   path, or designed per letter?
3. Where letters connect: should joins be part of the designed stroke
   (one path per word), or planned as now? What does Apple do between
   letters of "hello"?
4. Is there a case for keeping the outline as a *target* (e.g. to fit the
   stroke's width profile against it per letter, offline) while rendering
   only the stroke?
5. Anything in our stroke-order recovery or hand editor worth keeping as the
   authoring tool for designed strokes.

## 9. How to look at the current state

- Run: `npm install`, `npm run dev`, open <http://localhost:5173>. Or the
  hosted build (no movie export): <https://andikleinke.github.io/handwriter/>.
- Pick a font, type a word, Analyze; the debug view shows the recovered pen
  paths with stroke numbers; the A–Z panel is the per-letter editor; the
  version number in the title lists every tag.
- In-page diagnostics: `const I = await import('/scripts/inspect.js')`;
  `I.setup('autography')`, `I.setText('he')`, `I.metrics()`,
  `I.atArc([...], box)` (frames at arc lengths), `I.mapZoom(...)` (the
  reveal map with provenance and late ink marked), `I.profile(chain, a, b)`
  (per-sample readings with edge/facing flags).
- `README.md` holds the full design log (every mechanism above has a
  paragraph with the numbers); `docs/research-even-script.md` is the
  penmanship research behind the retired pen model;
  `docs/plan-font-fit.md` is a retired plan.
- Attached by the owner for context: `hello-hevc-alpha.mov`, an export of
  "hello" in Autography from v0.26.0.
